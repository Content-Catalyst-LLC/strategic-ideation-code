# Modeling Principles for Strategic Ideation

## 1. Treat scores as prompts for judgment

Weighted scoring can discipline comparison, but it should not be treated as objective truth. Scores depend on frames, criteria, evidence, values, and institutional context.

## 2. Make assumptions visible

Every idea rests on assumptions about behavior, capability, resources, timing, legitimacy, and system response. Useful models expose assumptions rather than hiding them.

## 3. Preserve rejected alternatives

Rejected alternatives often contain institutional memory. Documenting why an idea was rejected prevents repetitive rediscovery and improves future judgment.

## 4. Separate idea quality from evidence quality

A promising idea may have weak evidence. A familiar idea may have strong feasibility but limited strategic value. Models should distinguish these dimensions.

## 5. Use portfolio logic

Strategic ideation should compare immediate actions, prototypes, research questions, contingent options, and long-term pathways together.

## 6. Include ethics and distributional effects

An idea is not strategically mature if it ignores who bears cost, risk, exclusion, or implementation burden.

## 7. Build revision into the process

The goal is not to lock in the first selected idea. The goal is to build a system that learns as evidence changes.
