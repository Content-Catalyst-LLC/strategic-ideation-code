# Modeling Principles for Strategic Ideation

## 1. Treat ideas as portfolio candidates

Ideas should not be evaluated only as isolated proposals. A professional ideation system asks what role each idea plays in the larger portfolio: infrastructure, prototype, learning probe, governance mechanism, implementation bridge, risk audit, or strategic filter.

## 2. Separate idea quality from evidence quality

A promising idea may have weak evidence. A familiar idea may have strong feasibility but low strategic value. Separating idea quality from evidence strength protects strategy from false confidence.

## 3. Make assumptions explicit

Every strategic idea rests on assumptions about behavior, institutions, resources, legitimacy, timing, incentives, and system response. The model should expose these assumptions and assign test methods.

## 4. Use option architecture

A mature portfolio distinguishes time horizon, reversibility, commitment level, dependencies, sequencing value, scenario robustness, and portfolio fit.

## 5. Prototype before overcommitment

Ideas with high uncertainty or high assumption risk should be tested through low-cost learning probes before major resource commitment.

## 6. Preserve decision memory

Rejected alternatives, rationale, criteria, assumptions, and feedback should be preserved so learning can accumulate.

## 7. Include legitimacy and burden review

Strategic ideas are incomplete if they ignore who is affected, who has voice, who bears implementation burden, and who can contest the decision.

## 8. Build learning governance

Feedback should not remain at the project level. It should revise assumptions, criteria, option architecture, and future strategic choices.
