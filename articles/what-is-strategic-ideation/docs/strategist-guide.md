# Strategist Guide

This scaffold is designed for professional strategic ideation work. It supports seven recurring strategy questions:

1. **Frame:** Are we defining the right problem?
2. **Generate:** Are we creating a rich enough possibility space?
3. **Structure:** Are ideas organized into useful option architecture?
4. **Evaluate:** Are criteria, evidence, uncertainty, and assumptions explicit?
5. **Prototype:** What can be tested before major commitment?
6. **Translate:** How would a selected idea become an implementation pathway?
7. **Learn:** How will feedback revise future ideation and strategy?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Score the portfolio

Use `idea_portfolio.csv` and the Python workflow to compare ideas across strategic fit, feasibility, systems leverage, learning value, ethical legitimacy, knowledge reusability, uncertainty, and assumption risk.

### Step 2: Review option architecture

Use `option_architecture.csv` to classify ideas by role, time horizon, commitment level, reversibility, dependency complexity, portfolio fit, scenario robustness, and sequencing value.

### Step 3: Surface assumptions

Use `assumptions.csv` to identify high-criticality, low-confidence assumptions. These should become prototype or review priorities.

### Step 4: Review evidence strength

Use `evidence_register.csv` to separate strong evidence from weak, contested, old, or low-relevance evidence.

### Step 5: Build prototype plans

Use `prototype_plan.csv` to convert uncertain ideas into learning probes.

### Step 6: Map implementation pathways

Use `implementation_pathways.csv` to assess owner clarity, governance fit, feedback strength, dependency load, and scaling risk.

### Step 7: Preserve decision memory

Use the templates in `docs/templates/` to record decisions, rejected alternatives, assumptions, and review triggers.

## Professional facilitation questions

- What problem frame makes this idea visible?
- What alternative frame would make a different idea visible?
- What must be true for this idea to work?
- Which assumptions are high-criticality and low-confidence?
- What evidence supports the idea?
- What evidence would disconfirm the idea?
- Is this idea an immediate action, prototype, strategic filter, governance system, or future option?
- What is the lowest-cost useful test?
- What feedback would cause us to revise the idea?
