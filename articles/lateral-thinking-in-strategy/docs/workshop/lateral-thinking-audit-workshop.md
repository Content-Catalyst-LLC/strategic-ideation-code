# Lateral Thinking Audit Workshop

## Purpose

Help a strategy team determine whether it is genuinely challenging the problem frame or merely generating unusual ideas inside the same assumptions.

## Suggested duration

2 to 3 hours.

## Participants

- Strategy lead
- Facilitator
- Domain expert
- Cross-domain thinker
- Systems thinker
- Stakeholder or ethics representative
- Implementation owner
- Knowledge architecture lead

## Agenda

### 1. State the dominant frame

Name the current problem definition, key metrics, categories, constraints, and implied solution space.

### 2. Surface assumptions

List the assumptions that must be true for the current frame to remain valid.

### 3. Identify frame origin

Ask whether the frame came from history, leadership preference, metrics, technology, professional habits, regulation, or stakeholder experience.

### 4. Apply lateral interventions

Use provocation, reversal, random entry, forced association, concept extraction, and escape.

### 5. Reconstruct the new frame

Translate lateral moves into reframed problem statements.

### 6. Test systems fit

Review feedback, incentives, constraints, second-order effects, and implementation dynamics.

### 7. Review stakeholders and political safety

Ask who benefits from the old frame, who is excluded, and whether people can challenge assumptions safely.

### 8. Integrate with convergence

Define criteria, evidence, prototype tests, risks, stakeholder review, and decision memory.

## Output

- dominant-frame statement
- assumption register
- lateral-move log
- reframed problem statements
- systems-fit notes
- stakeholder legitimacy review
- convergence-integration plan
- decision-memory record
