# Modeling Principles for Strategy, Tactics, and Ideation

## 1. Diagnose by layer before prescribing action

Do not assume a visible execution problem is only a tactical problem. It may originate in weak ideation, ambiguous strategy, poor translation, feedback failure, or legitimacy gaps.

## 2. Treat scoring as structured judgment, not mechanical truth

Scores reveal comparison logic. They do not replace deliberation, evidence review, stakeholder judgment, or ethical assessment.

## 3. Separate strategy from planning

A plan lists actions. Strategy explains why those actions belong together and what tradeoffs define the chosen direction.

## 4. Preserve rejected alternatives

A professional strategy system should record what was rejected and why. Rejected alternatives are part of decision memory.

## 5. Route feedback to the right layer

Tactical feedback may require tactical correction, strategic revision, or conceptual reframing. Feedback becomes useful only when it reaches the layer that can act on it.

## 6. Include legitimacy and burden review

Strategic coherence is incomplete if affected stakeholders, implementation burdens, accountability, and distributional effects are invisible.

## 7. Design for adaptation without incoherence

Adaptive systems need revision pathways, but they also need a stable logic of direction. Strategy should learn without dissolving into reactivity.
