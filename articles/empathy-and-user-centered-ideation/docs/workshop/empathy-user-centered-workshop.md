# Empathy and User-Centered Ideation Workshop

## Purpose

Help a strategy team turn empathy into disciplined inquiry, reframing, ideation, prototype learning, and decision-making.

## Suggested duration

2 to 3 hours.

## Participants

- Strategy lead
- Design researcher
- Service or product owner
- Frontline representative
- Stakeholder or community representative
- Systems thinker
- Ethics or accessibility reviewer
- Decision-memory owner

## Agenda

### 1. Name the current problem frame

Identify what the organization currently believes the problem is.

### 2. Identify projection risk

Ask where internal assumptions may be substituting for user reality.

### 3. Map the stakeholder field

Include direct users, non-users, implementers, affected publics, intermediaries, future users, and hidden-burden groups.

### 4. Review lived-experience evidence

Examine interviews, observations, support logs, journey maps, accessibility signals, and workarounds.

### 5. Map journey friction

Identify time cost, cognitive load, emotional cost, trust risk, uncertainty, and dropoff points.

### 6. Identify unmet needs

Separate visible requests from hidden needs and burdens.

### 7. Compare preference and behavior

Ask where stated preference diverges from observed behavior.

### 8. Reframe the problem

Write a revised problem statement grounded in empathic evidence.

### 9. Add ethics and systems review

Map burden shifts, representation gaps, power, consent, redress, and system feedback.

### 10. Connect to decisions

Define what evidence changes prototypes, priorities, budgets, implementation, or governance.

## Output

- stakeholder field map
- observation synthesis
- journey friction map
- unmet-need register
- preference-behavior comparison
- reframed problem statement
- ethics and systems review
- prototype-test plan
- decision traceability record
