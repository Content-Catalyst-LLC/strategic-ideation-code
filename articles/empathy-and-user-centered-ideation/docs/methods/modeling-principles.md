# Modeling Principles for Empathy and User-Centered Ideation

## 1. Treat empathy as evidence work

Empathy is not a feeling substituted for analysis. It is a structured way to collect and interpret lived-experience evidence.

## 2. Reduce projection risk

The institution's model of a problem should be tested against the user's lived model.

## 3. Look for hidden burden

Time cost, cognitive load, emotional stress, trust risk, accessibility burden, and repeated explanation often reveal unmet need.

## 4. Distinguish preference from behavior

What people say, what they do, what they avoid, and what they work around should be compared.

## 5. Use empathy to reframe the problem

The highest value of user-centered inquiry is often a better strategic question.

## 6. Expand from users to stakeholders

Direct users, non-users, implementers, affected publics, intermediaries, and future users may all matter.

## 7. Add systems thinking

User-centered improvements can shift burden elsewhere. Feedback loops, incentives, and system constraints must be reviewed.

## 8. Link insight to decisions

Empathy becomes strategic only when it changes priorities, prototypes, budgets, governance, implementation, or revision.
