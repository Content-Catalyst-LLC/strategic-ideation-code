# Modeling Principles for Content Frameworks in Strategic Ideation

1. Content frameworks should improve strategic judgment, not merely presentation consistency.
2. Frameworks differ from templates because they encode purpose, logic, use, governance, and review standards.
3. Content type definition prevents idea records, briefs, memos, maps, playbooks, and narratives from being confused.
4. Conceptual hierarchy separates purpose, principles, themes, problems, opportunities, options, decisions, and actions.
5. Required fields should match the decision the content supports.
6. Evidence and assumptions should remain visible.
7. Narrative coherence should preserve problem, stakes, insight, direction, mechanism, evidence, and choice.
8. Modular content components support reuse and recombination.
9. Framework governance prevents version drift, quality decay, and AI-generated ambiguity.
10. Ethical framework design protects stakeholder voice, burden visibility, dissent, and uncertainty.
