# Heuristic Audit Workshop

## Purpose

Help a strategy team identify which shortcuts shaped idea generation before options were evaluated.

## Suggested duration

2 to 3 hours.

## Participants

- Strategy lead
- Facilitator
- Domain experts
- Cross-domain reviewers
- Stakeholder or ethics representative
- Implementation owner
- Systems thinker
- Knowledge architecture lead

## Agenda

### 1. Reconstruct the idea search

Identify how the ideas were generated, who participated, which prompts were used, and when the search stopped.

### 2. Name the heuristics

List the shortcut families used: availability, representativeness, anchoring, recognition, satisficing, affect, default, social proof, expertise, stakeholder, systems, analogy, or first principles.

### 3. Identify anchors and stopping rules

Ask what first framed the search and why the team stopped generating ideas.

### 4. Score search breadth

Review idea diversity by frame, source domain, stakeholder visibility, mechanism, novelty, and system level.

### 5. Audit institutional shortcuts

Identify templates, dashboards, approval rules, and metrics that shaped what counted as realistic.

### 6. Test complexity fit

Ask whether the heuristics assume a stable, linear environment or whether they account for feedback, delay, adaptation, and second-order effects.

### 7. Select interventions

Choose practices that widen the next search.

### 8. Preserve decision memory

Record rejected, deferred, and unexplored options, plus what would reopen them.

## Output

- heuristic inventory
- anchor log
- search-breadth scorecard
- institutional shortcut audit
- complexity-fit review
- stopping-rule memo
- intervention plan
- decision-memory record
