# Strategist Guide

This scaffold is designed for professional heuristic-aware ideation work. It supports nine recurring questions:

1. **Search logic:** Which shortcuts generated the idea space?
2. **Closure pressure:** Did anchoring and satisficing end the search too early?
3. **Recognition trap:** Were familiar ideas favored because they were easy to explain?
4. **Institutional shortcuts:** Which routines, templates, metrics, or approval rules defined realism?
5. **Search breadth:** Did ideas vary by frame, source domain, stakeholder, mechanism, and system level?
6. **Expertise:** Did expert compression improve quality or enclose the search?
7. **Complexity fit:** Does the heuristic fit the environment?
8. **Stopping rules:** Why did the team stop searching?
9. **Memory:** Which rejected and deferred ideas should be preserved?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Score the heuristic context

Use `heuristic_contexts.csv` and the Python workflow to assess availability, anchoring, recognition, satisficing, affect, default gravity, social proof, exploratory diversity, stakeholder variation, source-domain diversity, systems checks, political safety, and decision memory.

### Step 2: Review heuristic use

Use `heuristic_use_cases.csv` to determine which shortcuts are valuable, risky, context-appropriate, or in need of replacement.

### Step 3: Score search breadth

Use `idea_search_inventory.csv` to evaluate whether generated ideas differ by dominant heuristic, frame, source domain, stakeholder visibility, novelty, and system level.

### Step 4: Audit institutional shortcuts

Use `institutional_shortcuts.csv` to identify planning rituals, templates, metrics, and leadership filters that may be shaping ideation.

### Step 5: Test complexity fit

Use `complexity_fit.csv` to determine whether a shortcut assumes a simpler environment than the one being addressed.

### Step 6: Govern stopping rules

Use `stopping_rules.csv` to assess why the search ended and what evidence would reopen it.

### Step 7: Select interventions

Use `intervention_library.csv` to choose practices such as multiple starting prompts, independent generation, distant analogy libraries, stopping-rule audits, institutional shortcut audits, stakeholder burden heuristics, systems-fit checks, and decision-memory archives.
