# Modeling Principles for Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

## 1. Name the uncertainty correctly

Risk, uncertainty, ambiguity, complexity, and ignorance require different decision methods.

## 2. Avoid false precision

Use ranges, assumptions, sensitivity analysis, and confidence levels rather than pretending uncertain estimates are exact.

## 3. Compare frames before comparing options

A decision framed as risk control, opportunity, resilience, public value, or capability preservation will reveal different tradeoffs.

## 4. Separate option generation from option evaluation

Organizations often evaluate familiar options too early. Strong processes widen the option set before scoring.

## 5. Make assumptions explicit

Every uncertain strategy depends on assumptions. Assumptions should be documented, owned, monitored, and revised.

## 6. Stress test across futures

Decisions should be evaluated across plausible scenarios, not only against a preferred forecast.

## 7. Preserve option value

Staged commitments, reversibility, modularity, and exit paths protect learning and future choice.

## 8. Use experiments to produce evidence

Prototypes, pilots, simulations, and shadow tests can generate information before full commitment.

## 9. Govern uncertainty ethically

Uncertainty does not excuse opacity. Decision-makers should disclose uncertainty, review burden, and define revision responsibilities.

## 10. Preserve decision memory

Record the question, frame, options, assumptions, evidence, uncertainty, triggers, rationale, and revision history.
