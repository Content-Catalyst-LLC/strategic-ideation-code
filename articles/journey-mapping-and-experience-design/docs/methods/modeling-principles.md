# Modeling Principles for Journey Mapping and Experience Design

## 1. Treat journeys as sequences

The quality of a journey depends on the order, continuity, timing, and connection of stages, not only on isolated touchpoints.

## 2. Model accumulated friction

Small burdens become strategic problems when they accumulate across stages.

## 3. Treat transitions as high-risk zones

Context loss, handoff failure, repetition, and status gaps often occur between touchpoints.

## 4. Include emotional and trust signals

An experience is shaped by confidence, uncertainty, dignity, privacy, reassurance, and perceived legitimacy.

## 5. Build accessibility into every stage

Accessibility is not an end-stage compliance check. It shapes entry, navigation, decision-making, support, recovery, and completion.

## 6. Connect frontstage and backstage

Visible experience depends on hidden data, roles, staffing, governance, workflows, and decision systems.

## 7. Use journey mapping to reframe problems

A journey map should challenge assumptions about where the real problem sits.

## 8. Convert maps into decisions

Journey maps become strategic when they affect priorities, redesigns, budgets, metrics, and institutional learning.
