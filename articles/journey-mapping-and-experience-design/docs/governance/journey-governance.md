# Journey Mapping and Experience Design Governance

Journey governance ensures that journey maps influence redesign, implementation, measurement, and learning rather than becoming static artifacts.

## Governance requirements

- Journey scope statement
- Stakeholder and channel map
- Evidence source inventory
- Stage friction register
- Touchpoint review
- Transition-risk register
- Accessibility and dignity review
- Trust and status review
- Decision-pathway review
- Service blueprint overlay
- Redesign priority record
- Measurement and revision plan
- Experience learning record

## Review cadence

- Before redesign: review journey evidence, scope, and excluded groups
- Before prototyping: review friction, transitions, accessibility, trust, and decision pathways
- Before implementation: review backstage capacity and governance alignment
- Before scale: review accessibility, equity risk, and burden shifts
- During implementation: monitor dropoff, support signals, trust, and access
- After implementation: archive learning and revision decisions

## Anti-failure rules

- Do not optimize isolated touchpoints while transitions fail.
- Do not treat journey maps as decorative diagrams.
- Do not ignore non-users and early abandonment.
- Do not treat accessibility as late compliance.
- Do not improve one journey by shifting burden to another group.
- Do not claim journey success from completion metrics alone.
- Do not let frontstage promises exceed backstage capacity.
