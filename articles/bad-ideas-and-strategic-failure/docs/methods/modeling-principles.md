# Modeling Principles for Bad Ideas and Strategic Failure

1. Bad ideas are diagnosable before they become expensive commitments.
2. A bad idea is not merely unpopular; it has weaknesses that threaten strategic judgment, implementation, legitimacy, learning, or responsibility.
3. Problem-frame integrity must be tested before solution enthusiasm.
4. Confidence should not exceed evidence and tested learning.
5. Causal mechanisms should be specific enough to test.
6. Implementation readiness must include capacity, roles, incentives, dependencies, trust, and governance.
7. Incentive failure can turn well-intentioned ideas into harmful systems.
8. Hidden burden and ethical invisibility are strategic failure risks.
9. Power-protected ideas require sponsor-independent review.
10. AI fluency can make weak ideas look more mature than they are.
11. Stop rules and revision triggers are essential for responsible experimentation.
12. Strategic failure learning must distinguish idea failure from execution failure.
