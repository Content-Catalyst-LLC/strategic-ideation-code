# Boundary Setting Workshop

## Purpose

Help a strategy team make problem boundaries explicit, test them against alternative frames, and design boundary revision triggers before option selection or implementation.

## Suggested duration

2 to 3 hours.

## Participants

- Strategy lead
- Facilitator
- Systems thinker
- Stakeholder researcher
- Implementation owner
- Data or evidence lead
- Ethics or governance reviewer
- Decision-memory owner

## Agenda

### 1. State the current boundary

Name what is inside the current problem frame and what is outside it.

### 2. Map the wider system

Identify relationships, feedback loops, institutions, constraints, and consequences.

### 3. Review stakeholder inclusion

Identify decision-makers, implementers, users, non-users, affected communities, indirect stakeholders, and future stakeholders.

### 4. Compare causal frames

Generate at least three plausible causal explanations and the ideas each would produce.

### 5. Extend the time horizon

Compare immediate, short-term, medium-term, long-term, and future consequences.

### 6. Distinguish authority and consequence

Map what the team can decide, influence, coordinate, escalate, and monitor.

### 7. Review evidence boundaries

Identify missing forms of knowledge and weak interpretation assumptions.

### 8. Conduct ethical boundary review

Ask who benefits, who is excluded, what harms are externalized, and what redress exists.

### 9. Test boundary-sensitive options

Evaluate whether options remain strong under alternative boundaries.

### 10. Define revision triggers

Record signals that require the boundary to change.

## Output

- boundary statement
- system context map
- stakeholder boundary map
- causal-frame comparison
- temporal horizon review
- institutional responsibility map
- evidence boundary review
- ethical boundary note
- option sensitivity review
- revision trigger log
- decision-memory record
