# Strategist Guide

This scaffold supports game-theoretic strategy through ten recurring questions:

1. **Players:** Who can act, react, block, enable, imitate, defect, coordinate, or change the rules?
2. **Payoffs:** What does each actor value?
3. **Moves:** What strategies are available to each actor?
4. **Information:** Who knows what, and when?
5. **Timing:** Who moves first, who observes, and who responds?
6. **Equilibrium:** What outcome is likely to stabilize under current incentives?
7. **Cooperation:** What makes cooperation stable or fragile?
8. **Signals:** What does each move communicate, and how might it be misread?
9. **Mechanisms:** Can incentives, rules, standards, defaults, verification, or governance be redesigned?
10. **Ethics:** Who bears cost, risk, constraint, or exclusion?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Score interaction profiles

Use `strategic_interaction_profiles.csv` to evaluate rivalry, coordination potential, information asymmetry, retaliation risk, institutional support, behavioral realism, mechanism-design potential, and ethical complexity.

### Step 2: Map actor payoffs

Use `actor_payoffs.csv` to analyze material payoffs, reputation, legitimacy, risk avoidance, control preferences, cooperation dependence, exit options, and power.

### Step 3: Review signals

Use `information_signals.csv` to analyze credibility, observability, costliness, ambiguity, response sensitivity, misinterpretation risk, and coordination value.

### Step 4: Diagnose equilibrium

Use `equilibrium_diagnoses.csv` to evaluate desirability, stability, efficiency, fairness, exit pressure, coordination barriers, incentive misalignment, and rule-change need.

### Step 5: Review cooperation fragility

Use `cooperation_fragility.csv` to evaluate trust, monitoring, defection temptation, retaliation feasibility, reputation value, reciprocity, enforcement, and shared upside.

### Step 6: Review repeated interaction

Use `repeated_interactions.csv` to evaluate interaction frequency, future shadow, reputation visibility, memory, proportional retaliation, forgiveness, and trust repair.

### Step 7: Design mechanisms

Use `mechanism_design.csv` to evaluate incentive alignment, transparency, verification, enforcement, participation, burden distribution, adaptability, and implementation feasibility.

### Step 8: Add behavioral realism

Use `behavioral_game_theory.csv` to evaluate fairness, trust, identity, bounded reasoning, loss aversion, norms, and legitimacy.

### Step 9: Review ethics and power

Use `ethics_power.csv` to evaluate power asymmetry, voice, exit, transparency, burden shifting, manipulation, legitimacy, and redress.

### Step 10: Preserve strategic interaction memory

Use `strategic_interaction_memory.csv` to document players, payoffs, information, equilibrium logic, signals, mechanisms, ethics, revisions, and reuse value.
