# Modeling Principles for Game Theory and Strategic Interaction

## 1. Strategy is interdependent

The value of a move depends on what others can do, believe, expect, signal, infer, and change in response.

## 2. Identify the real players

Include actors who can respond, block, enable, imitate, coordinate, defect, regulate, or redesign the rules.

## 3. Map payoffs on the actors' terms

Payoffs may include profit, influence, legitimacy, reputation, risk avoidance, control, public value, trust, safety, or mission success.

## 4. Treat information as strategic

Information asymmetry, credibility, signaling, observability, ambiguity, and misinterpretation shape response.

## 5. Diagnose equilibrium

Ask what outcome is likely to stabilize under current incentives, even if that outcome is inefficient or unfair.

## 6. Test cooperation fragility

Cooperation requires trust, monitoring, enforcement, reciprocity, reputation, and a manageable temptation to defect.

## 7. Use repeated interaction deliberately

Reputation, memory, future shadow, retaliation, forgiveness, and trust repair can make cooperation more plausible.

## 8. Consider mechanism design

Sometimes the strongest move is to redesign incentives, rules, defaults, verification, participation, and information flows.

## 9. Integrate behavioral realism

Fairness, trust, identity, bounded rationality, loss aversion, norms, and legitimacy affect strategic behavior.

## 10. Review ethics and power

Game design can support cooperation, but it can also manipulate, exclude, coerce, obscure risk, or shift burdens.
