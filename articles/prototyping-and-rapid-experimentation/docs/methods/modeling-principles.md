# Modeling Principles for Prototyping and Rapid Experimentation

## 1. Treat prototypes as questions

A prototype should ask a specific question of reality. It is not a decoration, demo, or proof of virtue.

## 2. Test critical assumptions first

The most valuable tests focus on assumptions that are both uncertain and consequential.

## 3. Match fidelity to learning purpose

Use the lowest useful fidelity. Overbuilt prototypes slow learning and increase attachment.

## 4. Define evidence before testing

Teams should know what would support, challenge, or fail to answer the hypothesis before the experiment begins.

## 5. Observe behavior, not only opinion

Approval, enthusiasm, and stakeholder preference do not equal adoption or feasibility.

## 6. Interpret prototypes systemically

Prototype success in a narrow test may conceal feedback effects, capacity problems, incentive shifts, and burden transfers.

## 7. Govern experiments ethically

Speed cannot justify harm, privacy risk, exclusion, manipulation, or participant burden.

## 8. Link learning to decisions

Experimentation becomes strategic only when evidence changes what the organization continues, revises, stops, scales, or studies next.
