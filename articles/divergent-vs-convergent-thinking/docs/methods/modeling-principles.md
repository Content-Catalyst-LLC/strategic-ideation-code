# Modeling Principles for Divergent vs Convergent Thinking

## 1. Treat ideation as structured search

Divergent thinking expands the search space. Convergent thinking reduces it through criteria, constraints, evidence, and judgment.

## 2. Separate modes before integrating them

Generation and evaluation require different norms. Mixing them too early can suppress weak but promising ideas.

## 3. Diagnose premature convergence

Premature convergence occurs when closure pressure is high and exploratory breadth is low.

## 4. Diagnose unbounded divergence

Unbounded divergence occurs when exploration is high and evaluative discipline is weak.

## 5. Classify constraints

Real constraints should be respected. Assumed constraints should be challenged before convergence.

## 6. Audit criteria for bias

Selection criteria can reproduce power, familiarity, institutional inertia, or metric convenience.

## 7. Include affected stakeholders before selection

Stakeholders should influence criteria and selection, not only react to decisions after convergence.

## 8. Preserve decision memory

Document why ideas were advanced, rejected, deferred, or revised. This prevents lost learning across cycles.
