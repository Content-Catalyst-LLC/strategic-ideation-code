# Strategist Guide

This scaffold is designed for professional divergence-convergence strategy work. It supports eight recurring questions:

1. **Mode:** Are we generating, developing, evaluating, selecting, or implementing?
2. **Divergence:** Has the idea space been expanded enough?
3. **Convergence:** Are criteria, constraints, and selection rules clear?
4. **Constraints:** Which limits are real, assumed, uncertain, or redesignable?
5. **Selection integrity:** Are ideas selected by evidence and strategy or by power and familiarity?
6. **Stakeholders:** Who influences criteria and selection?
7. **Iteration:** How does evidence reopen the process?
8. **Memory:** Why were ideas advanced, rejected, deferred, or revised?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Score the ideation context

Use `ideation_contexts.csv` and the Python workflow to assess exploratory breadth, evaluative discipline, iteration quality, constraint clarity, stakeholder inclusion, evidence contact, action readiness, decision memory, and closure pressure.

### Step 2: Review risks

Use the premature convergence and unbounded divergence outputs to identify process imbalance.

### Step 3: Score idea portfolios

Use `idea_portfolio.csv` to compare ideas by novelty, strategic fit, evidence, feasibility, stakeholder value, risk visibility, ethical legitimacy, readiness, and assumption burden.

### Step 4: Audit criteria

Use `evaluation_criteria.csv` to evaluate definition clarity, measurement validity, bias risk, stakeholder relevance, and strategic importance.

### Step 5: Classify constraints

Use `constraint_register.csv` to separate real constraints from assumed constraints.

### Step 6: Review selection integrity

Use `evaluation_rounds.csv` to examine mode clarity, participation, transparency, evidence use, stakeholder voice, documentation, and iteration triggers.

### Step 7: Strengthen learning

Use `learning_cycles.csv` and `stakeholder_review.csv` to improve iteration and legitimacy before implementation.
