# Co-Design Governance

Co-design governance ensures that participation is meaningful, accessible, reciprocal, power-aware, decision-linked, and accountable.

## Governance requirements

- Participation purpose statement
- Stakeholder and affected-group map
- Influence boundary statement
- Access and support plan
- Reciprocity review
- Power-risk review
- Method fit plan
- Accountable synthesis protocol
- Conflict and tradeoff register
- Decision traceability record
- Accountability and close-the-loop plan
- Participatory learning-memory record

## Review cadence

- Before participation: review purpose, stakeholders, influence, access, reciprocity, and ethics
- During participation: monitor power, access, safety, conflict, and inclusion
- After synthesis: review interpretation, participant validation, tradeoffs, and missing voices
- Before decision: trace participant input to options, changes, rationale, and implementation
- After decision: close the loop and preserve learning

## Anti-failure rules

- Do not call it co-design if participants cannot influence decisions.
- Do not invite people after the problem frame and solution are already locked.
- Do not ask participants to contribute labor without reciprocity.
- Do not treat one participant as representative of an entire group.
- Do not erase conflict to manufacture consensus.
- Do not translate lived experience into strategy without accountability.
- Do not collect stories without a response path.
- Do not disappear after participation ends.
