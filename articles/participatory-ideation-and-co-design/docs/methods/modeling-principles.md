# Modeling Principles for Participatory Ideation and Co-Design

## 1. Treat participation as influence, not attendance

Participation is meaningful only when participants can shape framing, ideas, criteria, prototypes, implementation, governance, or learning.

## 2. Map affectedness and exclusion

The most important stakeholders are not always the most visible, powerful, available, or institutionally familiar.

## 3. Make influence boundaries explicit

Participants should know what is open, what is constrained, who decides, and how input will be used.

## 4. Design participation for access

Language, disability, schedule, technology, compensation, transportation, care obligations, psychological safety, and cultural context shape who can participate.

## 5. Account for power

Institutional authority, professional jargon, facilitation choices, and interpretive control can reproduce hierarchy inside participation.

## 6. Integrate knowledge carefully

Lived experience, technical expertise, operational knowledge, systems analysis, evidence, and strategic constraints should be integrated without erasing difference.

## 7. Preserve conflict and tradeoffs

Co-design should not manufacture false consensus. Disagreement, burden shifts, and value tensions should be documented.

## 8. Close the loop

Participants should know what changed, what did not, why decisions were made, and what future influence or challenge is possible.
