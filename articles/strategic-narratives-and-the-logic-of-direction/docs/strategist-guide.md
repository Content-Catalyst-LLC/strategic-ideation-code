# Strategist Guide

This scaffold is designed for professional strategic narrative work. It supports nine recurring strategy questions:

1. **Situation:** What is happening now?
2. **Diagnosis:** Why is it happening, and what does it mean?
3. **Purpose:** What value or responsibility guides direction?
4. **Choice:** What path is being chosen over alternatives?
5. **Sacrifice:** What must be refused, delayed, or abandoned?
6. **Sequence:** How does action unfold over time?
7. **Role:** Who contributes what to the direction?
8. **Accountability:** Do actions confirm the story?
9. **Drift:** Has the narrative changed meaning or lost discipline?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Score narrative coherence

Use `narrative_profiles.csv` and the Python workflow to assess diagnosis, purpose, choice, sequencing, roles, future credibility, evidence, accountability, stakeholder visibility, and ethical visibility.

### Step 2: Review narrative elements

Use `narrative_elements.csv` to inspect the quality of situation, diagnosis, choice, sequence, role, and future-state statements.

### Step 3: Map direction logic

Use `direction_logic.csv` to test alignment among diagnosis, purpose, choice, sequence, roles, and future.

### Step 4: Audit narrative-performance gaps

Use `performance_evidence.csv` to compare the narrative against budgets, decisions, metrics, implementation, and stakeholder evidence.

### Step 5: Check role alignment

Use `role_alignment.csv` to identify whether teams understand their contribution and decision authority.

### Step 6: Review stakeholder interpretation

Use `stakeholder_interpretations.csv` to identify perceived truthfulness, inclusion, burden visibility, and contestation.

### Step 7: Govern drift

Use `narrative_drift_events.csv` and `narrative_governance.csv` to repair fragmentation, slogans, ethical blindness, or brittle heroic narratives.

## Professional facilitation questions

- What situation does this narrative describe?
- What diagnosis is it asking people to accept?
- What future does it make plausible?
- What choices and sacrifices does it require?
- What actions would contradict it?
- Who is included in the story, and who is absent?
- What evidence would require revision?
