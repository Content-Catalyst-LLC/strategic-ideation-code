# Modeling Principles for Strategic Narratives and Direction

## 1. Treat narrative as strategic logic, not messaging

Messaging communicates the narrative. It does not replace diagnosis, purpose, choice, sequence, role, and accountability.

## 2. Test the diagnosis

A narrative must explain causes, structures, stakes, and evidence. A weak diagnosis produces weak direction.

## 3. Require choice and sacrifice

A narrative becomes strategic only when it clarifies what is being prioritized and what is being refused, deferred, or sacrificed.

## 4. Map sequence and dependency

Direction unfolds over time. Narrative should explain what comes first, what depends on what, and where learning or revision occurs.

## 5. Check role logic

People need to know how their work contributes to the direction and what authority or feedback channels they have.

## 6. Audit narrative-performance gaps

Actions, budgets, metrics, roles, and institutional behavior must be compared against the story being told.

## 7. Include stakeholder interpretation

A narrative that sounds coherent internally may fail externally if affected groups experience it as untruthful, exclusionary, or burden-shifting.

## 8. Govern narrative drift

Narratives can become slogans, fragment across teams, narrow into metrics, or become detached from evidence. Drift requires review.
