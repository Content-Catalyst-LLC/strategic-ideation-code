# Modeling Principles for Scenario Planning and Futures Thinking

## 1. Explore plausibility, not prediction

Scenarios are not forecasts. Their purpose is to structure uncertainty and improve judgment across multiple plausible futures.

## 2. Start with a focal question

Scenario work should be tied to a decision, domain, uncertainty, or strategic issue. Without a focal question, scenarios may become interesting but unusable.

## 3. Use consequential uncertainties

Strong scenarios focus on uncertainties that are both important and difficult to predict.

## 4. Build internally coherent futures

Each scenario should explain how drivers and uncertainties combine into a plausible future environment.

## 5. Stress test strategy

Scenarios should test assumptions, reveal fragility, identify robust strategies, and inform adaptive pathways.

## 6. Monitor signals

Scenario work should produce indicators that help teams notice when one pathway may be emerging.

## 7. Review ethics and representation

Futures are imagined from particular positions. Scenario work should ask whose futures are included, who bears burden, and who benefits.

## 8. Preserve scenario memory

Scenario work should document focal questions, drivers, uncertainties, scenario logic, implications, signals, decisions, and remaining uncertainty.
