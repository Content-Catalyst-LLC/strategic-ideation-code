# Modeling Principles for Creative Constraints and Innovation

## 1. Treat constraints as design conditions

Constraints are not only barriers. They can focus attention, reveal tradeoffs, protect legitimacy, and force recombination.

## 2. Classify before judging

Separate real, temporary, assumed, political, ecological, and ethical constraints before using them in convergence.

## 3. Avoid romanticizing scarcity

Scarcity can stimulate creativity, but it can also create burnout, fragility, exclusion, and undercapacity.

## 4. Distinguish rigidity from focus

Focus helps innovation. Rigidity suppresses adaptation. The same boundary can do either depending on context and capability.

## 5. Include stakeholders in constraint definition

Affected groups often know how constraints are experienced and where hidden burdens fall.

## 6. Track dynamic constraints

Constraints change through technology, policy, ecology, legitimacy, funding, and institutional learning.

## 7. Link constraint to capability

A constraint is productive only when the organization has enough learning capacity to work through it.

## 8. Preserve decision memory

Document why constraints were respected, challenged, redesigned, or treated as non-negotiable.
