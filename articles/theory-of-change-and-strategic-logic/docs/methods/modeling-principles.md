# Modeling Principles for Theory of Change and Strategic Logic

## 1. Treat strategic logic as a causal argument

A strategic idea should explain how action is expected to become change.

## 2. Separate activities from outcomes

Delivering workshops, dashboards, platforms, or reports is not the same as producing outcomes.

## 3. Test causal links one by one

A theory of change is only as strong as its weakest causal links.

## 4. Map assumptions at every arrow

Every arrow in a theory-of-change diagram contains assumptions about behavior, capacity, evidence, context, and system response.

## 5. Match evidence to the claim

Evidence that an activity occurred does not prove behavior change, outcome movement, or long-term impact.

## 6. Include actors as part of the mechanism

Stakeholders, implementers, decision-makers, users, partners, and resistors interpret and alter the strategy.

## 7. Add systems thinking

Feedback loops, delays, incentives, adaptation, and burden shifts can change the pathway.

## 8. Build learning loops

A theory of change becomes strategic when evidence can revise, pause, scale, or stop the strategy.
