# Modeling Principles for Strategic Communication and Conceptual Coherence

1. Strategic communication is part of strategy, not merely final messaging.
2. Conceptual coherence requires stable meaning across audiences, artifacts, decisions, and implementation.
3. Broad concepts need definitions, boundaries, mechanisms, examples, exclusions, and version history.
4. Narrative coherence organizes complexity without hiding tradeoffs, uncertainty, or dissent.
5. Audience adaptation should change the route into the idea, not the core meaning of the idea.
6. Communication claims should remain traceable to evidence, assumptions, confidence, and counterevidence.
7. Decision alignment prevents messages from implying commitments that governance has not made.
8. Implementation translatability tests whether strategic language can become roles, routines, resources, and measures.
9. Strategic listening treats audience confusion, resistance, and reinterpretation as learning evidence.
10. Ethical communication preserves stakeholder voice, burden visibility, dissent, redress, and accountability.
