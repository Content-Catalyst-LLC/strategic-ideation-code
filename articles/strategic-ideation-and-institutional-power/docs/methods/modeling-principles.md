# Modeling Principles for Strategic Ideation and Institutional Power

1. Strategic ideas do not compete in neutral conditions.
2. Agenda-setting determines what an institution is allowed to imagine.
3. Feasibility claims should be tested as power-shaped judgments, not accepted as neutral facts.
4. Sponsorship can protect good ideas, but it can also lower scrutiny for preferred ideas.
5. Evidence standards should be applied consistently across power-aligned and power-challenging ideas.
6. Participation should be evaluated by actual influence, not only invitation or consultation.
7. Dissent should be preserved as strategic evidence, not softened into generic feedback.
8. Classification systems should make burden, harm, dissent, legitimacy, and future consequences visible.
9. Resource allocation and incentives select ideas before formal decisions do.
10. AI-assisted ideation should expand what institutions can see, not polish what they already prefer.
