# Modeling Principles for Ethics of Strategic Ideation

1. Ethics enters strategic ideation at problem framing, not only at implementation.
2. Problem frames distribute responsibility and shape which solutions appear realistic.
3. Stakeholder voice must be evaluated by influence, not merely by consultation.
4. Evidence claims should preserve source quality, uncertainty, counterevidence, and transfer limits.
5. Burden distribution should be made visible before strategy asks less powerful groups to carry costs.
6. Power and realism should be reviewed as strategic claims, not accepted as neutral constraints.
7. Uncertainty should trigger monitoring, reversibility, stop rules, and revision triggers.
8. Long-term responsibility should include future stakeholders, ecosystems, institutional capacity, and lock-in.
9. AI-assisted ideation requires disclosure, traceability, bias review, stakeholder review, and human accountability.
10. Redress and accountability must be designed before harms occur, not improvised afterward.
