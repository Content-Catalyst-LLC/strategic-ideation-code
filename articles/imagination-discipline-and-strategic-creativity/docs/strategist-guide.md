# Strategist Guide

This scaffold is designed for professional strategic creativity work. It supports ten recurring questions:

1. **Novelty depth:** What is actually new?
2. **Relevance:** Does the idea matter for the strategic problem?
3. **Coherence:** Does the idea hold together conceptually?
4. **Mechanism:** How would the idea produce change?
5. **Constraint:** Which constraints are productive, and which are inherited assumptions?
6. **Stakeholders:** Whose experience shaped the idea, and whose burden is hidden?
7. **Systems:** What feedback, incentives, delays, dependencies, and second-order effects matter?
8. **Evidence:** How can the idea be tested without overcommitting?
9. **Maturation:** What would make the idea stronger?
10. **Memory:** What should be preserved for future learning?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Score the creative idea

Use `creative_ideas.csv` and the Python workflow to evaluate novelty, relevance, coherence, mechanism clarity, testability, stakeholder grounding, systems fit, developmental potential, implementation risk, and revision capacity.

### Step 2: Review novelty depth

Use the novelty-theater risk score to separate meaningful strategic creativity from new language around old assumptions.

### Step 3: Audit constraints

Use `constraint_audit.csv` to distinguish productive constraints from dead constraints.

### Step 4: Ground the idea in stakeholders

Use `stakeholder_reviews.csv` to evaluate burden, trust, agency, participation, legitimacy, and hidden harm.

### Step 5: Test systems fit

Use `systems_fit.csv` to assess feedback, incentives, dependencies, delays, second-order effects, boundaries, adaptation, and robustness.

### Step 6: Design evidence pathways

Use `evidence_pathways.csv` to define what would strengthen or weaken the idea.

### Step 7: Manage maturation and portfolio decisions

Use `idea_maturation.csv` and `creative_portfolio.csv` to decide whether an idea should be advanced, tested, revised, archived, or reopened later.
