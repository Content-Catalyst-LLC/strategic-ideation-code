# Modeling Principles for Leverage Points in Systems Change

## 1. Separate visibility from sensitivity

The most visible problem location is not always the highest-leverage intervention point.

## 2. Classify the intervention level

Identify whether the intervention acts on parameters, buffers, delays, feedback loops, information flows, rules, goals, or paradigms.

## 3. Score structural depth and governance need together

Deep leverage without legitimacy, monitoring, and decision memory can become destabilizing.

## 4. Treat feedback as a core mechanism

A leverage point is powerful when it changes what accumulates, stabilizes, or reproduces over time.

## 5. Treat information as behavior-shaping

Visibility changes perception, and perception changes response. Information design requires governance.

## 6. Diagnose the operating goal

Systems often serve goals different from their stated values. The real goal is visible in budgets, metrics, authority, and incentives.

## 7. Surface paradigms carefully

Paradigm work requires conceptual clarity, coalition, evidence, transition pathways, and practice embedding.

## 8. Pair leverage with learning

Every leverage hypothesis should include early-warning indicators, revision triggers, and decision memory.
