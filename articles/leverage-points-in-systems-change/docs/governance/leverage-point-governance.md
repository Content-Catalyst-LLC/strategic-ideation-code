# Leverage-Point Governance

High-leverage interventions require governance because they can redirect behavior widely.

## Governance requirements

- Leverage hypothesis
- Intervention-level classification
- Feedback-loop review
- Information-flow review
- Rule and incentive review
- Operating-goal diagnosis
- Paradigm review
- Tipping-threshold review
- Second-order effect review
- Early-warning indicator set
- Revision-trigger log
- Decision-memory record

## Review cadence

- Before ideation: define recurring pattern and symptom-structure distinction
- Before option selection: classify leverage depth and governance need
- Before implementation: review feedback, information, rules, goals, paradigms, and thresholds
- During implementation: monitor early-warning indicators and unintended consequences
- Before scale: confirm legitimacy, learning capacity, and revision triggers
- After implementation: update decision memory and revise leverage hypothesis

## Anti-failure rules

- Do not confuse visibility with leverage.
- Do not rely on parameters when the system problem is structural.
- Do not change information flows without governance.
- Do not change incentives without countermetrics.
- Do not change goals without aligning budgets, metrics, and decision rights.
- Do not attempt paradigm change through rhetoric alone.
- Do not scale high-leverage interventions without learning loops.
