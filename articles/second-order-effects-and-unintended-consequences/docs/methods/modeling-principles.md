# Modeling Principles for Second-Order Effects and Unintended Consequences

## 1. Separate first-order gain from system-level outcome

Immediate success can coexist with delayed harm.

## 2. Map propagation pathways

Every intervention travels through relationships, workflows, incentives, narratives, technologies, and institutions.

## 3. Treat actors as adaptive

People respond to interventions through interpretation, resistance, compliance, gaming, imitation, and workarounds.

## 4. Look for feedback loops

The system may amplify the intervention, dampen it, resist it, or redirect it.

## 5. Review burden shifts

A strategy can solve a problem for one group while transferring cost, risk, labor, or emotional burden to another.

## 6. Assess fragility

Efficiency, automation, centralization, and metrics can reduce slack, redundancy, trust, optionality, and recovery capacity.

## 7. Build early-warning indicators

Delayed effects require leading signals and decision-linked thresholds.

## 8. Preserve decision memory

Record expected first-order effects, possible second-order effects, assumptions, evidence, signals, and revision triggers.
