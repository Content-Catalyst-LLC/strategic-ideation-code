# Modeling Principles for Strategic Foresight and Long-Term Thinking

## 1. Prepare rather than predict

Strategic foresight is not a point forecast. It improves preparedness across plausible futures.

## 2. Expand temporal depth

Evaluate decisions across immediate, short, medium, long, and intergenerational horizons.

## 3. Structure uncertainty

Drivers, signals, critical uncertainties, scenarios, and adaptive pathways make uncertainty usable.

## 4. Interpret weak signals carefully

Weak signals should neither be dismissed as noise nor overinterpreted as certainty.

## 5. Stress test strategy

Strategies should be assessed across futures, not only optimized for a baseline expectation.

## 6. Account for path dependence

Present choices shape future options through lock-in, transition cost, capability gaps, and governance constraints.

## 7. Preserve option value

Staged commitments, modular design, reversibility, and trigger conditions protect future choice.

## 8. Govern anticipation

Foresight requires decision rights, review cadence, evidence standards, signal ownership, ethical review, and learning memory.

## 9. Include futures ethics

Responsible foresight asks whose futures are imagined, who bears future burden, and how intergenerational responsibility is handled.

## 10. Preserve learning memory

Foresight should document focal questions, signals, drivers, uncertainties, scenarios, decisions, pathways, and remaining uncertainty.
