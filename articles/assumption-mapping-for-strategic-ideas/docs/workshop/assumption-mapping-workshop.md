# Assumption Mapping Workshop

## Purpose

Help a strategy team identify which assumptions must be tested before an idea becomes a major commitment.

## Suggested duration

2 to 3 hours.

## Participants

- Strategy lead
- Facilitator
- Evidence or analytics lead
- Stakeholder researcher
- Implementation owner
- Governance owner
- Ethics reviewer
- Decision-memory owner

## Agenda

### 1. State the strategic idea

Clarify the idea, intended outcome, target stakeholders, and decision context.

### 2. Map the strategic logic

Describe how the idea is expected to move from action to participation, behavior, outcome, and impact.

### 3. List assumptions by category

Capture problem, need, behavior, capacity, technology, market, institutional, system, evidence, ethical, and future-facing assumptions.

### 4. Identify hidden assumptions

Ask what the team is taking for granted because of habit, leadership narrative, professional expertise, existing metrics, stakeholder exclusion, or speed pressure.

### 5. Score criticality and uncertainty

Prioritize assumptions that are both load-bearing and weakly evidenced.

### 6. Review evidence quality

Check whether evidence is reliable, relevant, transferable, timely, and interpreted carefully.

### 7. Design tests

Match tests to assumptions. Avoid testing only what is easy.

### 8. Review prototypes

Ensure each prototype tests a critical assumption and has a decision use.

### 9. Define revision triggers

Record what evidence strengthens, weakens, revises, pauses, or stops the idea.

### 10. Archive decision memory

Record what was believed, why it was believed, what was tested, and what changed.

## Output

- assumption inventory
- criticality/uncertainty map
- evidence review
- priority tests
- prototype learning plan
- theory-of-change assumption review
- option confidence review
- revision trigger log
- decision-memory record
