# Modeling Principles for Mental Models in Strategic Thinking

## 1. Treat strategy as interpreted reality

Strategy is formed through models, not raw data. Every model defines what counts as evidence, what counts as risk, and what counts as plausible action.

## 2. Separate model quality from model confidence

A model can be confidently held and still be wrong. Confidence should never be treated as evidence of environmental fit.

## 3. Audit causal assumptions

Strategists should explicitly ask whether a model assumes linear, mechanistic, probabilistic, systems, institutional, game-theoretic, or participatory causality.

## 4. Watch for model monoculture

A single dominant model can make an organization appear rigorous while narrowing what it can see.

## 5. Make disconfirmation actionable

A model should specify what evidence would force revision. If no evidence can revise it, it is not a strategic model; it is a belief system.

## 6. Include stakeholder and ethical visibility

A model that cannot see affected people, unequal burdens, exclusion, or legitimacy risk is strategically incomplete.

## 7. Preserve model revision history

Decision memory should record not only what the organization chose, but what model of reality made that choice appear sensible.

## 8. Stress-test models under future conditions

Scenario planning should test the governing model, not merely the plan.
