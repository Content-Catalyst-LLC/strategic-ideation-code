# Strategist Guide

This scaffold supports feedback-loop design through ten recurring questions:

1. **Learning question:** What uncertainty does the feedback loop address?
2. **Signal:** Where will feedback come from?
3. **Quality:** Is the signal relevant, reliable, timely, representative, interpretable, and behaviorally rich?
4. **Interpretation:** Who will make meaning from the signal?
5. **Adjustment:** How can feedback change the design or strategy?
6. **User grounding:** Does feedback reflect real experience, behavior, access, trust, and workarounds?
7. **Time:** Are delayed effects, drift, and accumulated patterns tracked?
8. **Systems:** What feedback loops, incentives, burdens, and capacity limits matter?
9. **Ethics:** Is feedback collected and used responsibly?
10. **Memory:** How will learning be preserved and reused?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Score feedback systems

Use `feedback_systems.csv` to compare signal quality, interpretation capacity, adjustment speed, user insight, stability, ethics, systems awareness, decision linkage, and learning memory.

### Step 2: Review signals

Use `signals.csv` to assess feedback sources and signal quality.

### Step 3: Review interpretation capacity

Use `interpretation_capacity.csv` to examine contextual understanding, domain expertise, user research capacity, systems thinking, bias review, triangulation, and decision relevance.

### Step 4: Review adjustment pathways

Use `adjustment_pathways.csv` to determine whether feedback can become responsible change.

### Step 5: Review user feedback

Use `user_feedback.csv` to distinguish observed behavior from stated preference and include non-users.

### Step 6: Review temporal learning

Use `temporal_learning.csv` to track delayed outcomes, drift, patterns, cadence, and overreaction risk.

### Step 7: Review systems impact

Use `systems_impact.csv` to assess feedback risk, delays, burden shifts, capacity, incentives, scale, and monitoring.

### Step 8: Review ethics

Use `ethical_governance.csv` to evaluate privacy, consent, accessibility, burden, representation, redress, accountability, and traceability.

### Step 9: Review decision linkage

Use `decision_linkage.csv` to connect feedback to authority, resources, revision triggers, stop rules, scale rules, and implementation paths.

### Step 10: Preserve feedback memory

Use `feedback_memory.csv` to archive signals, interpretation, change records, decision rationale, remaining uncertainty, and reusable learning.
