# Modeling Principles for Opportunity Recognition and Evaluation

## 1. Opportunity is relational

An opportunity is not just an external condition. It is a relationship between conditions, capabilities, timing, interpretation, and strategic purpose.

## 2. Separate signal from story

Strong opportunity evaluation distinguishes credible signals from hype, anecdotes, sponsor preference, and noise.

## 3. Capability matters

A promising opportunity is not actionable unless the organization has, can build, or can access the capability required to pursue it.

## 4. Timing changes opportunity quality

Opportunities can be early, timely, late, premature, or expired. Timing should be evaluated explicitly.

## 5. False positives and false negatives both matter

Over-pursuing weak opportunities and missing real opportunities are different errors with different costs.

## 6. Learning value deserves protection

Some opportunities should be explored because they reduce uncertainty, preserve options, or improve future decisions.

## 7. Ethics and power are not optional

Opportunity framing shapes whose needs count, who benefits, who bears burden, and who gets to decide.

## 8. Use staged commitment

Avoid treating recognition as commitment. Use pilots, prototypes, stakeholder discovery, and evidence thresholds.

## 9. Preserve decision memory

Record signals, assumptions, evidence, capability gaps, timing, risk, ethics, decision gates, and revision triggers.

## 10. Portfolio thinking improves opportunity systems

Do not rely on a single opportunity. Balance exploratory, developmental, scaling, resilience, legitimacy, and option-preserving pathways.
