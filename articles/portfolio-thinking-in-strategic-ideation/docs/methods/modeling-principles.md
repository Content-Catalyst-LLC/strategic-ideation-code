# Modeling Principles for Portfolio Thinking in Strategic Ideation

## 1. Evaluate the set, not only the item

A portfolio is more than a list. It should reveal the pattern of roles, risks, learning, options, capacity, dependencies, ethics, and timing across ideas.

## 2. Assign strategic roles

Every idea should have a purpose in the portfolio: core improvement, exploration, option preservation, transformation, resilience, legitimacy, foresight, capability building, ethics governance, or legacy review.

## 3. Protect learning options

Ideas with high learning value should not be judged only by immediate return. Their purpose is to improve later decisions.

## 4. Respect capacity constraints

The portfolio must remain absorbable. Budget, staff time, leadership attention, technical capacity, governance bandwidth, stakeholder patience, and communication load all matter.

## 5. Sequence dependencies

Some ideas are foundations for others. Portfolio governance should identify readiness gaps before scaling visible bets.

## 6. Balance time horizons

Near-term progress should not crowd out medium-term capability, long-term adaptation, or intergenerational responsibility.

## 7. Avoid scoring theater

Scores should support judgment, not replace it. Use scoring to reveal questions and tradeoffs rather than create false precision.

## 8. Review ethics and power

Portfolio criteria can privilege some ideas and suppress others. Review sponsor power, stakeholder voice, burden distribution, transparency, and redress.

## 9. Prune and retire

A portfolio that cannot stop old ideas accumulates strategic debt. Retirement rules are part of responsible governance.

## 10. Preserve decision memory

Document roles, evidence, risk, dependencies, capacity, ethics, gates, revision history, and reuse value.
