# Strategist Guide

This scaffold supports portfolio thinking through ten recurring questions:

1. **Inventory:** What ideas are actually in play?
2. **Role:** What role does each idea play in the portfolio?
3. **Risk:** What exposure does each idea create?
4. **Learning:** What decision-relevant evidence does each idea produce?
5. **Time horizon:** When does value appear?
6. **Fit:** Does the idea belong in the strategy now?
7. **Capacity:** Can the organization absorb this commitment?
8. **Dependencies:** What must happen before or after this idea?
9. **Ethics:** Whose ideas, benefits, and burdens are represented?
10. **Governance:** How will ideas advance, change, stop, or retire?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Score strategic ideas

Use `strategic_ideas.csv` to compare impact, risk, learning value, option value, strategic fit, capacity demand, ethical resilience, evidence strength, dependencies, and governance readiness.

### Step 2: Review role balance

Use `portfolio_targets.csv` to compare the actual idea mix with a target strategic mix.

### Step 3: Evaluate risk and learning

Use `risk_learning_profiles.csv` to identify high-exposure ideas and high-learning options.

### Step 4: Test capacity

Use `capacity_load.csv` to determine whether the portfolio is absorbable.

### Step 5: Map dependencies

Use `dependencies.csv` to identify sequencing and readiness gaps.

### Step 6: Review time horizons

Use `time_horizons.csv` to avoid short-term overconcentration.

### Step 7: Review ethics and power

Use `ethics_power.csv` to evaluate sponsor bias, stakeholder voice, benefit concentration, burden distribution, and redress.

### Step 8: Strengthen governance

Use `governance_review.csv` to assess entry rules, cadence, pruning rules, evidence standards, decision rights, escalation paths, decision memory, and portfolio visibility.

### Step 9: Preserve decision memory

Use `decision_memory.csv` to document why ideas advance, pause, merge, or retire.
