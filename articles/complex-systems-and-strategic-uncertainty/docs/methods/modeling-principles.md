# Modeling Principles for Complex Systems and Strategic Uncertainty

## 1. Distinguish complicated from complex

Complicated systems may be decomposed. Complex systems generate behavior through interaction, adaptation, feedback, emergence, and history.

## 2. Treat uncertainty as structural when appropriate

Some uncertainty persists because the system evolves through interaction rather than because analysts lack sufficient data.

## 3. Model feedback explicitly

Every strategy should ask what loops it strengthens, weakens, creates, or misunderstands.

## 4. Anticipate adaptive actors

Competitors, users, stakeholders, regulators, communities, and teams may respond in ways that alter the intervention.

## 5. Watch path dependence

Legacy infrastructure, sunk costs, norms, trust memory, standards, and coordination structures constrain strategic possibility.

## 6. Use scenarios as disciplined stress tests

Scenarios should expose assumptions, test robustness, and guide adaptive pathways.

## 7. Design adaptive options

Prefer no-regret moves, low-regret experiments, hedges, modular commitments, reversible pilots, and staged pathways under deep uncertainty.

## 8. Build learning loops and decision memory

Implementation evidence should update assumptions, scenarios, options, and commitments.
