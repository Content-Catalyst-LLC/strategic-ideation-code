# Strategist Guide

This scaffold is designed for professional analogical transfer work. It supports nine recurring questions:

1. **Target:** What problem is being interpreted or solved?
2. **Source:** What domain, case, system, or model is being borrowed from?
3. **Mapping:** Which relations correspond between source and target?
4. **Breakpoints:** Where does the analogy fail?
5. **Adaptation:** How must the transferred idea change?
6. **Rivals:** What alternative analogies explain the target?
7. **Stakeholders:** Who is included, burdened, or erased by the analogy?
8. **Evidence:** What would validate or weaken the transfer?
9. **Memory:** Why was the analogy advanced, rejected, revised, or deferred?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Profile the strategy

Use `analogical_strategies.csv` and the Python workflow to assess structural fit, functional fit, surface distraction, adaptation quality, context sensitivity, stakeholder legitimacy, dynamic compatibility, innovation potential, and evidence strength.

### Step 2: Review source domains

Use `source_domains.csv` to evaluate relational clarity, mechanism visibility, constraint visibility, failure-mode visibility, prestige pressure, and transfer complexity.

### Step 3: Map source to target

Use `source_target_mappings.csv` to evaluate actor, relation, flow, constraint, feedback, failure-mode, and breakpoint correspondence.

### Step 4: Test adaptation

Use `adaptation_tests.csv` to evaluate constraint fit, stakeholder fit, mechanism fit, governance fit, implementation fit, evidence fit, and ethical fit.

### Step 5: Compare rival analogies

Use `rival_analogies.csv` to avoid locking into one comparison too early.

### Step 6: Review legitimacy

Use `stakeholder_legitimacy.csv` to detect burden invisibility, power-blind transfer, and low interpretive trust.

### Step 7: Track transfer hypotheses

Use `transfer_hypotheses.csv` to convert analogical insight into testable strategic learning.
