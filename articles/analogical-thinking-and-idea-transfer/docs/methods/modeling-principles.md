# Modeling Principles for Analogical Thinking and Idea Transfer

## 1. Treat analogy as structural transfer

A useful analogy transfers relations, mechanisms, feedback, constraints, and failure modes. It does not merely transfer vocabulary.

## 2. Separate source, target, mapping, and adaptation

Analogical work becomes strategic when each component is explicit.

## 3. Penalize surface distraction

A vivid comparison can be misleading when surface resemblance is high and relational fit is weak.

## 4. Define breakpoints

Every analogy breaks somewhere. Breakpoints should be documented before transfer becomes commitment.

## 5. Compare rival analogies

High-stakes decisions should not depend on one analogy. Rival analogies reveal hidden assumptions and alternative structures.

## 6. Test adaptation quality

The transferred idea must be modified for target constraints, governance, stakeholders, evidence, implementation, and ethics.

## 7. Include stakeholder interpretation

Affected groups may see the analogy differently. Their experience can reveal hidden burden, legitimacy gaps, or harmful framing.

## 8. Preserve decision memory

Document why an analogy was advanced, rejected, revised, or held for later.
