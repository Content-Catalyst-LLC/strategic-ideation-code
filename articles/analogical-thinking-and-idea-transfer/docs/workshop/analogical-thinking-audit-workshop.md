# Analogical Thinking Audit Workshop

## Purpose

Help a strategy team use analogy as disciplined structural reasoning rather than superficial comparison or prestige borrowing.

## Suggested duration

2 to 3 hours.

## Participants

- Strategy lead
- Domain expert
- Cross-domain expert
- Evidence or analytics lead
- Implementation owner
- Stakeholder or ethics representative
- Knowledge architecture lead
- Facilitator

## Agenda

### 1. Define the target problem

Clarify the target system, stakeholders, constraints, mechanisms, stakes, and uncertainty.

### 2. Identify source domains

List possible near, moderate, and far analogies.

### 3. Map structural correspondence

Compare actors, roles, flows, feedback, constraints, incentives, and failure modes.

### 4. Define breakpoints

Identify where each analogy fails or becomes misleading.

### 5. Compare rival analogies

Use multiple analogies to expose blind spots.

### 6. Adapt the transfer

Translate the source structure into the target context.

### 7. Review stakeholders and ethics

Ask who benefits, who is burdened, whose knowledge counts, and whether the analogy legitimizes harmful action.

### 8. Convert to evidence tests

Define hypotheses, prototypes, data needs, and revision triggers.

## Output

- target problem statement
- source-domain inventory
- source-target mapping table
- analogy breakpoints
- rival analogy comparison
- adaptation plan
- stakeholder legitimacy review
- transfer hypothesis register
- decision-memory record
