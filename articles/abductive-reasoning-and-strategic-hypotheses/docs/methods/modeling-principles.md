# Modeling Principles for Abductive Reasoning and Strategic Hypotheses

## 1. Treat hypotheses as provisional explanations

A strategic hypothesis should explain an observation, not merely name a preferred solution.

## 2. Preserve rival explanations

The first plausible explanation is often not the best one. Rival hypotheses protect against premature closure.

## 3. State the mechanism

A hypothesis without a mechanism is difficult to test or act on.

## 4. Define evidence before commitment

Evidence pathways should specify what would support and what would weaken the hypothesis.

## 5. Design disconfirmation deliberately

A hypothesis that cannot be weakened is not yet a useful strategic hypothesis.

## 6. Match commitment to confidence

Weak evidence may justify monitoring or low-cost research, not major irreversible action.

## 7. Use portfolios under uncertainty

Maintain core, rival, exploratory, weak-signal, and archived hypotheses.

## 8. Preserve decision memory

Record the observation, frame, hypotheses, evidence, decision, revision triggers, and archived learning.
