# Strategist Guide

This scaffold is designed for professional abductive reasoning work. It supports ten recurring questions:

1. **Observation:** What pattern, weak signal, anomaly, or failure requires explanation?
2. **Frame:** Which problem frame made this observation meaningful?
3. **Hypotheses:** What plausible explanations could account for it?
4. **Mechanism:** How would each hypothesis produce the observed pattern?
5. **Rivals:** What else could explain the same observation?
6. **Evidence:** What should we observe if the hypothesis is true?
7. **Disconfirmation:** What would weaken the hypothesis?
8. **Commitment:** What level of action is justified by current confidence?
9. **Revision:** What evidence would revise, split, replace, or archive the hypothesis?
10. **Memory:** What reasoning should be preserved for future learning?

## Recommended workflow

### Step 1: Start with the observation

Use `observations.csv` to document what needs explaining.

### Step 2: Compare hypotheses

Use `hypotheses.csv` to score rival explanations by explanatory strength, testability, evidence quality, strategic relevance, stakeholder visibility, systems fit, actionability, risk, reversibility, and confidence.

### Step 3: Design evidence pathways

Use `evidence_pathways.csv` to specify expected and weakening evidence.

### Step 4: Design disconfirmation tests

Use `disconfirmation_tests.csv` to protect against confirmation-seeking inquiry.

### Step 5: Match commitment to confidence

Use `commitment_levels.csv` to avoid overcommitting to weak hypotheses.

### Step 6: Govern revision

Use `revision_triggers.csv` to define learning conditions that reopen, revise, split, or archive hypotheses.

### Step 7: Manage the portfolio

Use `portfolio_status.csv` to maintain core, rival, exploratory, weak-signal, and archived hypotheses.
