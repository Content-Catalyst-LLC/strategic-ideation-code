# Modeling Principles for Risk, Tradeoffs, and Strategic Choices

## 1. Every meaningful strategy has cost

The goal is not to remove tradeoffs, but to make them explicit and decide which sacrifices are justified by which goals.

## 2. Do not treat risk as only financial exposure

Risk may be operational, reputational, regulatory, ethical, systemic, strategic, temporal, or political.

## 3. Surface opportunity cost

Ask what is displaced: capabilities, alternatives, learning, preparedness, legitimacy, flexibility, and future option value.

## 4. Compare across time horizons

A decision can look strong immediately while creating deferred fragility. Evaluate immediate, short-term, medium-term, long-term, and intergenerational effects.

## 5. Review reversibility and lock-in

The harder a decision is to reverse, the higher the evidence, governance, and review burden should be.

## 6. Stress test across scenarios

A strategy should be evaluated against stable, adverse, disruptive, resource-constrained, legitimacy-sensitive, and delayed-implementation futures.

## 7. Connect resources to stated priorities

Resource allocation reveals actual strategy. Budgets, staffing, attention, metrics, reserves, and learning budgets should match declared commitments.

## 8. Make value conflicts visible

Strategic choices are often declarations of value priority disguised as technical decisions.

## 9. Review burden and accountability

Ask who benefits, who pays, who bears risk, who has voice, and what redress or revision paths exist.

## 10. Preserve decision memory

Document the decision question, options, exposure, opportunity costs, value priorities, assumptions, triggers, and revision logic.
